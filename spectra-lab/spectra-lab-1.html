    How to read these charts
    • Spectrogram: time runs left to right, frequency bottom to top, color is energy. Stacked bright lines are partials — evenly spaced (harmonic) sounds pitched; stretched spacing sounds metallic; a smeared wash is noise.
    • The amber curve is the spectral centroid, the energy's centre of mass. Higher ≈ brighter. The violet curve is the 85% rolloff — almost all the energy lives below it.
    • Waveform: the attack shape is the punch; the outline is the sound's motion in time.
    • Spectrum: each peak is a partial. The left-to-right slope is warmth — steep means dark and warm, flat means bright or noisy. A raised floor between peaks is noise. Play the sound to watch the live spectrum move.
    • Flatness: near 0 the sound is tonal, near 1 it is noise. Many drum hits start noisy and become tonal as they ring.
